Kings Against Violence Initiative

KAVI is a nonprofit, 501(c)(3) youth-serving organization whose mission is to prevent and eliminate interpersonal violence from the lives of young people through advocacy, peer leadership, community mobilization, and social justice.

A public health approach

Violence is an endemic public health issue, particularly in low-income communities and communities of color. Gun violence is the leading cause of death among 15–24 year-olds. KAVI’s work in Central Brooklyn treats violence as both a symptom and a cause of inequity — and treats healing as community work. Hurt people hurt people.

Hospital intervention

At Kings County Hospital Center, a Level 1 trauma center, KAVI stands at bedsides and in family waiting rooms. Violence intervention specialists help young people cope with trauma, de-escalate conflict, and leave with a plan for health and safety — in partnership with the Emergency and Trauma Departments and Cure Violence, Inc.

School-based prevention

KAVI goes into schools so young people can prevent violence, heal from trauma, and navigate growing up. Peer facilitators from the neighborhood lead group learning, restorative justice, and advocacy with school staff. Parallel programs — KAVI Kings and KAVI Queens — support young men and young women.

Community healing

Programs also live outside the hospital and the classroom. KAVI works with a broad range of partners on community strengthening and healing, because safer pathways depend on education, mental health, and neighborhoods with resources — not on any one organization alone.

“We cannot do this work alone — it will take all of us working together to build a safer and stronger future for our young people.”

— KAVI · Kings Against Violence Initiative

Mayor’s Office of Community Safety

Building safer, stronger communities through prevention, partnership, and care. EMPOWER sits alongside the City’s key community-safety initiatives — the same network of outreach, crisis response, and survivor support that CVI teams already work in.

NYC’s Crisis Management System

A network of community-based organizations that works to prevent and reduce gun violence in the neighborhoods most affected by it. CMS partners deploy trained outreach workers and violence interrupters — credible messengers with deep roots who can de-escalate conflict, support people after incidents, and connect them to employment, education, mental health care, legal services, and other supports. CMS currently operates across 46 catchment areas and 29 NYPD precincts, with expansion into Bushwick, Harlem, and Washington Heights/Inwood.

B-HEARD

The Behavioral Health Emergency Assistance Response Division treats mental health crises as public health concerns. Teams of health professionals — EMTs and paramedics from FDNY and mental health professionals from NYC Health + Hospitals — respond to 911 mental health calls, a model launched in spring 2021.

Family Justice Centers

New Yorkers can walk into any of the City’s five Family Justice Centers for free, confidential help with domestic or gender-based violence, including intimate partner and family violence, elder abuse, sexual violence, stalking, or human trafficking. Civil legal, social, and criminal justice assistance sit under one roof. Anyone can walk in, regardless of age, income, gender identity, language, or immigration status.

PATH FORWARD

Partners Against the Hate (PATH) FORWARD launched in May 2021 under Local Law 49 to prevent and address hate crimes and bias-motivated incidents. The initiative supports more than 50 organizations that deliver prevention, community-based response, education, and safety across diverse communities, with additional grants for partners serving vulnerable populations.

Source: NYC Mayor’s Office of Community Safety — Key Initiatives
